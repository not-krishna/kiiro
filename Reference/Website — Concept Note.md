# **Kiiro — Concept Note**

### **A Creative Wellness Space, Rooted in Living Art**

---

## **Positioning Statement**

Kiiro is a creative wellness space where **traditional craft, contemporary art, and mindful practice converge** — inviting people to slow down, work with their hands, and reconnect: to culture, to community, and to themselves.

We believe creativity itself is a wellness practice. Not creativity as performance or product, but creativity as *process* — the same slow attention a master potter brings to the wheel is, at its core, a form of presence. Kiiro exists to make that experience accessible, structured, and shared.

**Working tagline options:**

* *Make. Feel. Belong.*  
* *Creative Wellness, Rooted in Craft*  
* *Where Making Meets Meaning*  
* *Hands On. Rooted. Restorative.* (evolution of current "Hands On. Rooted. Real.")

---

## **Why Now**

Two large, converging trends make this the right moment for Kiiro's repositioning:

**Wellness is going experiential.** India's wellness tourism market is valued at roughly USD 27.92 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach USD 38.22 billion by 2030 (Mordor Intelligence) — but the category is still dominated by passive formats: spas, yoga retreats, Ayurvedic stays. There is a visible gap for wellness that is *active*, *creative*, and *culturally specific* to India, rather than imported mindfulness formats.

**Craft is being rediscovered as therapy.** Pottery classes, ceramics studios, and "slow hobby" workshops have become one of the fastest-growing leisure categories globally — driven by screen fatigue, a hunger for tangible output in digital-first lives, and a broader "quiet living" cultural moment. India's living craft traditions give Kiiro a structural advantage no generic pottery studio has: centuries of technique, ritual, and story already built in.

Kiiro sits precisely at the intersection of these two trends — and currently, almost no brand in India owns that intersection clearly.

---

## **The Concept: What "Creative Wellness" Means at Kiiro**

Creative wellness is not a craft workshop with a wellness label attached, and it is not a meditation session with a paintbrush handed out. It is a **third category** — built on the belief that making, by hand, in the presence of tradition or self-expression, is itself a restorative act.

Kiiro delivers this through three pillars that every experience draws from:

### **1\. Traditional Art — *Root***

Living heritage craft, transmitted directly from master artisans: Jaipur blue pottery, Bagru block printing and natural indigo dyeing, Warli ritual painting, Mata ni Pachedi textile art, Cheriyal scroll painting. **What it offers:** lineage, patience, ritual, and the felt sense of continuing something ancestral rather than consuming it.

### **2\. Contemporary Art — *Express***

Modern creative practice that sits alongside heritage craft rather than replacing it: mixed-media and collage studios, ceramics as sculptural form, printmaking, urban sketching, contemporary curation and design-thinking sessions. **What it offers:** personal expression, experimentation, and relevance — proof that "creative" doesn't mean "historical."

### **3\. Wellness Practice — *Restore***

Mindful, embodied practice woven directly into the making process, not bolted on afterward: breathwork before a pottery session, silent making circles, nature-integrated studio settings, guided reflection and journaling, sound closings. **What it offers:** nervous-system regulation, presence, and a genuine sense of restoration — the reason someone leaves lighter than they arrived.

---

## **The Kiiro Method: Root → Create → Restore**

Every Kiiro experience — regardless of which pillar it draws from — moves through the same three-part arc:

| Stage | What Happens | Purpose |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Root** | Context, material, and story are introduced — the history of the craft, the hands that carried it here | Grounds the participant before they begin |
| **Create** | Hands-on making, guided by a master artisan or contemporary practitioner | Produces flow state and focused attention |
| **Restore** | Guided reflection, stillness, or a closing practice tied to the day's making | Converts the activity into a genuine wellness outcome |

This structure is what separates Kiiro from a craft class: **the making is the medium, but restoration is the outcome.**

## **Who It's For**

The audience segments stay largely the same as Kiiro's existing programming — but the *promise* to each shifts from "cultural education" to "creative wellness":

* **Individuals** seeking a genuine creative reset — a counter to burnout and screen fatigue, more active than a spa day, more grounded than a generic art class.  
* **Corporates & Organizations** wanting real team wellness, not another icebreaker — screen-free, tactile, quietly bonding.  
* **Hospitality & Luxury Resorts** wanting a signature wellness offering beyond spa menus — one with cultural depth and a story guests actually remember.  
* **Schools & Colleges** wanting creative and cultural literacy that also supports student wellbeing, not just skill-building.

## **The Ethical Anchor (Unchanged)**

Repositioning toward wellness does not dilute Kiiro's original commitment: every session channels fair, transparent income to the master artisans who lead it. Creative wellness for participants and dignified livelihood for artisans are designed as the same outcome, not competing priorities — this is also a genuine differentiator worth stating plainly in partner and investor conversations.
