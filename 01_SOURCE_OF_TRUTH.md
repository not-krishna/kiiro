## Cultural representation rule

The website must represent Indian craft traditions as **living
practices**, not museum objects.

Every artform page should prioritize:

-   people
-   hands
-   tools
-   materials
-   techniques
-   lineage
-   place
-   stories
-   teaching
-   contemporary relevance
-   the artisan's voice

The finished object alone is not enough.

The supplied brief explicitly asks for high-quality imagery of process,
materials, finished works, and artisans, especially "hands creating."

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Cultural integrity

### Never do this

-   invent a history for a craft
-   merge unrelated regional traditions
-   use a generic "tribal" label where a specific tradition is known
-   imply a craft is ancient without a source
-   assign a state/region without confirmation
-   call an artisan a "master" without client approval
-   create fake lineage
-   use AI-generated artisans as if they were real people
-   use AI-generated cultural artifacts as documentary evidence
-   claim an artisan community benefited financially unless documented
-   claim a wellness outcome as medical fact
-   use religious/cultural symbols purely as decoration without context

### Do this instead

If information is missing:

`TODO — client to provide verified lineage/history.`

If an image is illustrative:

`Illustration — not documentary photography.`

If an AI-generated visual is used:

`Conceptual visual only.`

Do not silently blur these distinctions.
